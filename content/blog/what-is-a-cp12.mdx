If you rent out a property in the UK with gas appliances, the CP12 — formally the
**Landlord Gas Safety Record (LGSR)** — is one of the few documents you genuinely cannot
afford to lose track of. This guide covers what it is, what the engineer actually checks,
and how to keep yours from lapsing.

## What a CP12 actually is

The CP12 is the written record of the annual gas safety check required by the
[Gas Safety (Installation and Use) Regulations 1998](https://www.hse.gov.uk/gas/landlords/index.htm).
The name comes from the old CORGI Proforma 12 — CORGI was replaced by the Gas Safe
Register in 2009, but the industry still calls the record a CP12.

Every gas appliance and flue that you provide for tenants must be checked by a
**Gas Safe registered engineer** every 12 months. The engineer records the results and
issues the certificate; you keep a copy and give one to your tenants.

## What the engineer checks

A gas safety check is not a boiler service — it is a safety inspection. For each
appliance the engineer will typically:

- check the appliance is burning gas correctly (operating pressure and heat input)
- confirm ventilation routes are adequate and unobstructed
- test that flues are removing combustion products safely
- check safety devices operate and cut off the supply when they should
- carry out a tightness test at the meter to rule out leaks

Each appliance gets a verdict. If something is unsafe the engineer will classify it —
**Immediately Dangerous (ID)**, **At Risk (AR)**, or **Not to Current Standards (NCS)** —
and issue a warning notice where needed.

## Who needs one

Any landlord letting residential property with gas appliances: private landlords,
letting agents managing on a landlord's behalf, HMO landlords, and holiday-let owners.
If the tenant owns the appliance (say, their own gas cooker), you must still have the
connecting pipework and flue checked.

## What happens if it lapses

Most lapsed CP12s are accidents — a reminder email in spam, or an assumption that the
letting agent had it covered. The consequences are real either way:

- **Enforcement.** The HSE can prosecute; penalties include unlimited fines and, in serious cases, imprisonment.
- **Possession risk.** A missing or invalid gas safety record can undermine your position in possession proceedings — especially significant since the Renters' Rights Act changes of May 2026 removed the Section 21 route.
- **Insurance.** Some landlord policies expect compliance with statutory obligations; a lapsed certificate can complicate a claim.

## How to stay compliant without a spreadsheet

The pattern behind almost every lapsed certificate is the same: the record lives in an
inbox, the renewal date lives in someone's head. Fix both:

1. **Store every certificate against the property**, not in an email thread.
2. **Book the renewal inside the final two months** — under the MOT-style rule you keep your original expiry date, so there is no reason to wait.
3. **Automate the reminder.** If your engineer's system schedules renewal reminders when the certificate is issued, nobody has to remember anything.

A good engineer will handle most of this for you — which is exactly why it is worth
asking yours how they issue and store their certificates.
